EPS firmware 3.0: executive summary

v3.0 replaces the network layer of the EPS with one written for the job. Where v2.2 needed a power cycle after the network failures we reproduced, v3.0 recovers by itself, and it records what happened when something goes wrong. It has passed every bench test so far; it has not yet run in the field.

Status 2026-10-09 · Bench: three units on a NETGEAR M4250, a switch model with field failures · All tests pre-registered, every failure reported

Bottom line

Field problem: EPS units on v2.2 randomly drop off the network (no ping, no web page, no control) until their power is cycled.

What we found: v2.2 has several defects that lead to exactly that state. The clearest one, measured this week: in DHCP mode, a single DHCP renewal that goes unanswered makes v2.2 restart itself, and if the DHCP server is away at that moment, the unit stays off the network until someone power-cycles it. Others: the network chip losing its settings (v2.2 never notices), and connections that block the control port. For units set to a static address, the field trigger is still open.

v3.0: recovers by itself from every one of these in our tests, typically within seconds, and keeps a log that will tell us what happened if a unit does drop off in the field. We are confident v3.0 is a large step up in network robustness. Whether it ends the field dropouts will be shown by a pilot, not by the bench.

What changed in v3.0

Outputs, timing, inputs, LEDs, the TCP control protocol (Companion, eXcontrol, Crestron, Q-SYS) and all stored settings work as before. A unit can be upgraded and downgraded without re-entering settings.

What we can say, and what we can't yet

We can say

  • v3.0 recovers by itself, in seconds, from every v2.2 failure we could provoke on it: DHCP outage, lost chip settings, stuck connections.
  • v3.0 behaves like a standard network device: standard DHCP, unique MAC, no stuck connections.
  • If a v3.0 unit ever drops off, its log will show why: link, chip, DHCP, address conflict or restart.

We can't say yet

  • That v3.0 ends the field dropouts: the trigger for static-mode units is still open.
  • How v3.0 handles the rare deaf state after a link negotiation: it hasn't occurred on v3.0 yet (0 in 300).
  • How v3.0 behaves with real loads switching, over days, and on other switch brands.
  • Results rest on three boards and two days of testing; the same team wrote v3.0 and the tests (mitigated by pre-registered criteria and crossover runs).

Recommended rollout

  1. Real box with real loads: v3.0 on our showroom unit, loads switched while the log runs. Any chip re-init in the log is a finding.
  2. Pilot: v3.0 with per-unit MACs on units at sites that had dropouts. With no failure in N unit-days, the 95 % upper bound on the failure rate is about 3/N (≈ 300 unit-days to show fewer than one failure per 100 unit-days).
  3. Site settings: static addresses outside DHCP ranges, or DHCP reservations; EPS on an untagged access port in the AV VLAN.
  4. On any dropout, before power-cycling: note the LED colour, check whether the unit's MAC is still in the switch's MAC table, then read NET_LOG after it is back.
  5. Fallback: v2.2 can be re-flashed without losing settings.
