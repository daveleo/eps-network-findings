Expromo EPS · KS0304 (ATmega328P + W5500) · v2.2 vs v3.0 and v3.1 (final)

Why EPS units drop off the network: what we know so far

Field symptom: a unit randomly stops answering ping, web page and TCP control until its power is cycled. Bench: two bare boards and one real EPS box (10 V supply, six SSRs, 22 µF reset capacitor) on a NETGEAR M4250; v3.1 and the standards suite on the real box, cabled to a PC. v3.0 is the network-layer rewrite; v3.1 (v3.0 + standards work) is the current final firmware. All tests pre-registered; every failure reported.

Status 2026-10-11 · full report: EPS Network Investigation

Where we stand

Established (measured)

  • DHCP mode: one unanswered DHCP renewal makes v2.2 restart itself; if the server is away during that restart, v2.2 stays off the network until power-cycled. v3.0 recovers by itself; v3.1 keeps asking (at most 64 s apart).
  • If the W5500 loses its settings, v2.2 is dead until reset; v3.1 is back in 2.1–6.9 s with the outputs kept on (10/10).
  • After some link negotiations a W5500 goes deaf (link up, transmits, receives nothing).
  • A USB/DTR reset also resets the W5500; v2.2's own watchdog restarts don't.
  • The Arduino Ethernet library writes its retry settings to the wrong W5500 registers.
  • Standards: v3.1 passes 46 of 56 pre-registered requirements, v2.2 26. Two failures shared by both are W5500 chip limits.

Open

  • What makes static-mode units drop off. Candidates: W5500 settings loss when real loads switch (not tested yet), deafness after a link negotiation (rare; can it last until a power cycle?).
  • Whether failing sites ran DHCP or static, and what their DHCP server did.
  • Why the M4250 dropped the v2.2 DHCP bindings although it ACKed their renewals.
  • v3.1 by hand (inputs, factory reset, LEDs, real power cycles), with real loads, and in the field.

Excluded or set aside

  • Socket starvation as the field cause: ping keeps working.
  • IP address conflicts (field knowledge).
  • Short mains interruptions on the real box: 18/18 recovered.
  • v2.2's own watchdog restarts making the chip deaf: 0 in 19+.
  • The NovaHub adapter (deaf at 100 Mbps on both boards): set aside as a false lead.

Single-register chip faults: which each firmware repairs (2026-10-11)

v3.1 checks five chip registers every 5 s and re-initialises if any is wrong. This test wrote one register fault at a time into the real box's own chip (a single-bit glitch is more likely than a full reset), 14 faults × 3 trials on each firmware, and watched every service for 120 s. Pre-registered; every trial reported. A fault passes only if all three trials recover with the registers restored and the outputs unmoved.

v3.1 repairs every register it checks (chip, IP, MAC, mask, gateway) in seconds with the outputs untouched, and survives every socket fault; v2.2 needs a power cycle for a chip reset, wrong IP or wrong mask, and keeps a wrong MAC or gateway for good. Four faults fall outside v3.1's check and leave both firmwares dead: the ping block, retry count 0, a forced 10-half link, and — the one that reproduces the field symptom exactly — PHY power-down.

A USB reset also resets the network chip, so this is the closest bench proxy for a power event. Neither firmware came up deaf; the deaf start-up has only ever appeared on one of the three boards (unit 2, 4 events).

Candidate v3.2 (built, under test)
Widens v3.1's 5 s check to the registers it now ignores — the mode register (ping block), the retry registers, and the PHY configuration (power-down and forced mode) — and re-initialises on a mismatch, as it already does for a bad address. The aim is to recover from the four faults above, including the PHY power-down that matches the field symptom. Built and compiles; being tested against the same fault matrix. v3.1 stays the final firmware until it proves out.
